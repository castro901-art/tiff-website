Abstract

We present CA-AALLM, a Trendify Labs research programme for culturally aware African language models. CA-AALLM investigates how language models can understand and generate language while accounting for the cultural context in which that language lives — including proverbs, idioms, honorifics, oral narratives, social registers and community knowledge.

The programme responds to a central limitation of contemporary language technology: systems trained and evaluated primarily on widely represented languages and written text can remain weak where language is low-resource, oral, dialectal or deeply situated in local practice. We argue that cultural context is not supplementary metadata for an African language model. It is part of the language problem itself.

This paper sets out the design principles, research scope, methodology, technical direction and evaluation framework for CA-AALLM. Our approach is community-first: consented documentation by and with native speakers, provenance-aware data stewardship, dialect- and code-switch-sensitive annotation, grounded retrieval, and evaluation designed with community input. The intended contribution is not translation at greater scale, but language systems that represent knowledge, register and meaning more faithfully for the communities they serve.

1. Introduction

Why cultural context is a modelling problem.

Large language models can process and generate human language at remarkable scale, yet their capability is not distributed equally. Performance varies across languages, dialects and communities, and fluency in a dominant language can conceal misunderstanding of the world in which another language is used.

For many East African communities, meaning is carried not only by vocabulary and grammar, but also by proverb, idiom, honorific, oral narrative, song, ecological knowledge and rules of social register. A model that translates words while missing these layers does not merely lose nuance; it can misrepresent people, practices and knowledge.

CA-AALLM is Trendify Labs’ programme to study and build for that fuller problem. We combine language documentation, annotation, model adaptation, retrieval of verified information and community-validated evaluation. Our central question is: what changes when cultural knowledge and community authority are treated as foundations of model design, rather than additions after training?

This paper makes three contributions:

  • It defines design principles for community-first African language research.
  • It describes a documented workflow from consent and oral documentation to transcription, annotation and validation.
  • It proposes an evaluation framework for cultural understanding, proverb interpretation and code-switched dialogue.

We present these as a programme and framework for research in progress; empirical claims will follow only from defined, reproducible evaluation.

2. Design principles

Six principles govern the CA-AALLM programme, from data collection to model behaviour.

Principle 1 — Community-first data

Data collection is based on Free, Prior and Informed Consent (FPIC), with community data sovereignty treated as a core principle.

Principle 2 — Oral-to-digital fidelity

Oral traditions are transcribed and annotated by native speakers before machine-learning processing.

Principle 3 — Grounded responses

Retrieval-augmented generation and a verified cultural knowledge base ground factual cultural responses.

Principle 4 — Code-switch awareness

Mixed-language examples reflect speech that moves between Swahili, English and mother-tongue languages.

Principle 5 — Dialect sensitivity

Dialect and register differences are considered separately rather than treated as interchangeable.

Principle 6 — Reversibility

Consent withdrawal and data provenance are built into the research design.

4. Methodology

From community engagement to validated data.

CA-AALLM follows a staged workflow in which cultural validation precedes model use. No stage treats community material as available by default.

  1. Community engagement. Engage communities and establish informed-consent arrangements before collection.
  2. Primary collection. Document oral histories, interviews, conversations and other consented material.
  3. Transcription. Prepare source-language transcripts with native-speaker involvement and review.
  4. Annotation. Add language, register, cultural-context and code-switch information where appropriate.
  5. Validation. Use community review to assess cultural accuracy before data is approved for use.

Data types under study

  • Oral histories and autobiographical narratives
  • Proverbs and idiomatic expressions with context
  • Daily conversation and code-switched dialogue
  • Traditional ecological knowledge
  • Governance and dispute-resolution discourse
  • Songs, oral poetry and other performance texts

Documentation and quality

  • Native-speaker transcription and correction
  • Unicode normalization for language-specific characters
  • Sentence-level language identification for mixed-language text
  • Community-specific entity and cultural-context annotation
  • Deduplication and privacy review
  • Source provenance and access-level tracking

5. Technical direction

Model adaptation and grounded retrieval.

Our technical direction adapts multilingual pretrained models to community language and cultural instruction, rather than treating the problem as translation alone.

Stage 1 — Language adaptation

Continue model training on community-language text to adapt representations to language patterns before task-specific training.

Stage 2 — Cultural instruction tuning

Use curated instruction-following data to support cultural questions, proverb interpretation and register-aware responses.

Grounding — Retrieval-augmented generation

Retrieve relevant information from a verified knowledge base and communicate uncertainty when reliable supporting material is unavailable.

The architecture above is presented as the programme’s research design. Implementation, training and deployment results will be reported in subsequent Trendify Labs technical publications.

6. Evaluation framework

Measuring what fluency alone cannot show.

A culturally aware model must be evaluated on more than generic language benchmarks. We propose evaluation of factual cultural understanding, proverb interpretation in context, and mixed-language comprehension and response naturalness. No scores are reported in this paper; the suites below define the intended measurement programme, to be validated with community input and transparent baselines.

CulturalQA-EA

Proposed evaluation of factual questions validated with community input.

ProverbInterp-EA

Proposed evaluation of proverb interpretation in cultural context.

CodeSwitch-EA

Proposed evaluation of mixed-language comprehension and response naturalness.

7. Ethics and governance

Community knowledge under community authority.

Cultural accuracy, consent and data sovereignty are requirements of the research, not constraints applied after it.

Research commitments

  • Use consented material and respect community data sovereignty.
  • Keep culturally restricted material out of public training and publication where required.
  • Do not invent cultural facts when verified support is unavailable.
  • Distinguish practices and language differences across communities.
  • Allow community review and correction within the documented governance process.

Publication boundaries

  • Private consent records and identifying informant details are not published.
  • Restricted, sacred or community-only knowledge is not reproduced publicly.
  • Internal operational configurations and non-public training details are omitted.
  • Only approved, public-facing research material is published.

8. Limitations and status

What this paper does and does not establish.

The present scope covers selected communities in Kenya and Tanzania and cannot represent the linguistic diversity of Africa. Oral and low-resource language research also presents distinct challenges in transcription, orthography, dialect coverage and annotation, which our methodology is designed to surface rather than conceal.

The evaluation suites in Section 6 are proposed frameworks. They require task definition, community validation, transparent baselines and reproducible protocols before they can support capability claims. Accordingly, this paper establishes a programme, a method and a measurement agenda — not a completed model, dataset, benchmark or result.

9. Conclusion

Towards language models that understand context.

CA-AALLM begins from a simple position: African languages cannot be served well by systems that treat culture as decoration around language. Through community-first documentation, provenance-aware stewardship, culturally grounded model adaptation and community-validated evaluation, Trendify Labs is building a research programme for language intelligence that is more faithful to how language is actually lived.

Our next publications will report progress on task and dataset definition, evaluation protocols and model studies as each is completed and validated. We invite research and community collaboration conducted under the governance principles set out above.
