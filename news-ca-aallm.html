Trendify Labs position paper

CA-AALLM: building context-aware African language model research

A position paper defining the research problem behind culturally aware African large language models and the work required before capability claims are justified.

Released 6 October 2026

The problem

Many language models are evaluated through broad benchmarks that do not adequately test African languages, cultural context, community knowledge or local forms of expression. The central problem is not simply that a language is missing from a model. It is that the evaluation may fail to ask whether the model understands the world in which the language is used.

Trendify Labs' position

Trendify Labs positions CA-AALLM as a research programme connecting two layers: a human and contextual layer involving communities, language and culture; and a model layer involving training, evaluation and deployment. The programme begins with problem definition and measurement, not with a claim that a new model is already better.

What we will research

  • Define culturally and linguistically grounded tasks with community partners.
  • Build datasets with provenance, consent, subgroup and context metadata.
  • Compare models using transparent baselines and disaggregated metrics.
  • Evaluate factuality, reasoning, translation, safety and cultural appropriateness.
  • Publish failure cases and limitations alongside improvements.

Evidence boundary

The supplied context dataset contains five community records, two language families, five language codes and 15 county associations. This is a schema and coverage starting point. It is not a benchmark, training corpus or model evaluation, and it does not establish language capability.

What this paper does not claim: It does not claim that Trendify has solved this problem, that the supplied data is complete, or that a model performs well without a defined benchmark and reproducible evaluation.

What success would mean

Success would mean a credible, reproducible evaluation programme that makes capability gaps visible and guides model development toward systems that are more useful, safer and more accountable in African contexts.
