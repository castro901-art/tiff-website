Culturally Aware African AI Language Model

A research programme focused on language, cultural context, oral traditions and knowledge systems across selected East African communities.

Research status: This page describes the programme and methods documented in the research handbook. It does not claim that planned training, datasets or benchmarks have been completed.
01 / Overview

AI grounded in language and cultural context

The CA-AALLM handbook describes a culturally aware African language model intended to understand and generate language while accounting for cultural context, including proverbs, idioms, honorifics, oral narratives and social registers.

The research responds to gaps that can arise when AI systems have limited representation of low-resource languages, oral knowledge and local context. The programme aims to reduce cultural misrepresentation and improve how systems handle language variation and community-specific knowledge.

The handbook frames the work as more than translation: it aims to represent the knowledge systems and context of the communities included in the research.

Research focusLanguage, cultural knowledge and responsible model development.

The programme brings together documentation, annotation, model adaptation, retrieval of verified information and evaluation.

02 / Research approach

Core design principles

The following principles are taken from the handbook’s model design section and expressed here as public-facing summaries.

PRINCIPLE 01

Community-first data

Data collection is based on Free, Prior and Informed Consent (FPIC), with community data sovereignty treated as a core principle.

PRINCIPLE 02

Oral-to-digital fidelity

Oral traditions are to be transcribed and annotated by native speakers before machine-learning processing.

PRINCIPLE 03

Grounded responses

Retrieval-augmented generation and a verified cultural knowledge base are intended to ground factual cultural responses.

PRINCIPLE 04

Code-switch awareness

Mixed-language examples reflect speech that moves between Swahili, English and mother-tongue languages.

PRINCIPLE 05

Dialect sensitivity

Dialect and register differences are considered separately rather than treated as interchangeable.

PRINCIPLE 06

Reversibility

The handbook specifies consent withdrawal and data provenance as important parts of the research design.

04 / Methodology

Documented research workflow

The handbook presents a staged collection and validation process. The public summary below avoids publishing private records or operational details.

Community engagement

Engage communities and establish informed-consent arrangements before collection.

Primary collection

Document oral histories, interviews, conversations and other consented material.

Transcription

Prepare source-language transcripts with native-speaker involvement and review.

Annotation

Add language, register, cultural-context and code-switch information where appropriate.

Validation

Use community review to assess cultural accuracy before data is approved for use.

Data types described

  • Oral histories and autobiographical narratives
  • Proverbs and idiomatic expressions with context
  • Daily conversation and code-switched dialogue
  • Traditional ecological knowledge
  • Governance and dispute-resolution discourse
  • Songs, oral poetry and other performance texts

Documentation and quality

  • Native-speaker transcription and correction
  • Unicode normalization for language-specific characters
  • Sentence-level language identification for mixed-language text
  • Community-specific entity and cultural-context annotation
  • Deduplication and privacy review
  • Source provenance and access-level tracking
05 / Technical direction

Model adaptation and grounded retrieval

The handbook proposes adapting a multilingual pretrained model rather than treating the project as a translation-only system.

STAGE 01

Language adaptation

Continue model training on community-language text to adapt representations to language patterns before task-specific training.

STAGE 02

Cultural instruction tuning

Use curated instruction-following data to support cultural questions, proverb interpretation and register-aware responses.

GROUNDING

Retrieval-augmented generation

Retrieve relevant information from a verified knowledge base and communicate uncertainty when reliable supporting material is unavailable.

These are methods described in the handbook, not a statement that a particular model, training run or retrieval system is already operational.

07 / Ethics and governance

Respect for community knowledge

The handbook treats cultural accuracy and community consent as central requirements of the research.

Public research commitments

  • Use consented material and respect community data sovereignty.
  • Keep culturally restricted material out of public training and publication where required.
  • Do not invent cultural facts when verified support is unavailable.
  • Distinguish practices and language differences across communities.
  • Allow community review and correction within the documented governance process.

Publication boundaries

  • Private consent records and identifying informant details are not published here.
  • Restricted, sacred or community-only knowledge is not reproduced on this public page.
  • Internal operational configurations and non-public training details are omitted.
  • Only approved, public-facing research material should be added to this section.
08 / Glossary

Research terminology

CA-AALLM

The abbreviation used for the Culturally Aware African AI Language Model in the project materials.

FPIC

Free, Prior and Informed Consent: the consent principle referenced in the handbook for community data collection.

RAG

Retrieval-Augmented Generation: retrieving supporting information from a knowledge source to ground generated responses.

LoRA

Low-Rank Adaptation, a parameter-efficient approach to adapting a pretrained model.

Code-switching

Using more than one language within a conversation or utterance.

Data provenance

Records describing where data came from and the permissions and handling rules associated with it.
